\subsection{What we would do differently}

Two things are already clear before the headline results exist, because they cost time during the
build rather than at the end.

\paragraph{Read the evaluator by running it, not by reading it.}
Every scoring decision in this work was settled by executing the official evaluator on constructed
cases with known answers. That was not the original plan, and the change of method was forced: a
conclusion reached by careful reading turned out to rest on wrong reasoning, and the behaviour that
mattered most --- extra predicted boxes being harmless on one image and ruinous across twenty ---
is invisible at the scale anyone would naturally test.

\paragraph{Treat every inherited constant as unverified.}
Of the code supplied to this project verbatim on the grounds that a subtle mistake would silently
corrupt results, two of five components contained such a mistake: a visual-token size belonging to a
different model generation, and an executor in which a bare string argument meant ``evidence label''
in some operations and ``numeric literal'' in others. Both produced plausible numbers rather than
errors. Neither was detectable without running the code against a case whose answer was known in
advance.

The common shape is worth stating once: in this field, the failures that cost weeks are not the ones
that crash. They are the ones that converge.
